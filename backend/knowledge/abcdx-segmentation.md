# ABCDX Segmentation

## Purpose of ABCD Segmentation

- **ABCD purpose**: Divide all paying customers into groups by margin and satisfaction.
- **Resource reallocation**: Reallocate resources from unprofitable customers (C/D) to profitable ones (A/B).

## Segment Descriptions

| Segment | Profile | Margin | Action |
|---------|---------|--------|--------|
| **A** (Awesome) | Maximally satisfied, buy quietly, no complex support | Highest | Focus segment for scaling |
| **B** (Basic) | Satisfied but not perfectly, product doesn't cover all needs | Acceptable, lower than A | Growth potential: move to A by improving product |
| **C** (Costly) | Bought "by mistake", constantly complaining, demand irrelevant features | Low or unprofitable (support costs) | Drop them |
| **D** (Dead) | Absolutely non-target, wreck team morale | Unprofitable | Actively "fire" or automate to zero cost |

## Segmentation Criteria

- **A/B**: Target margin (unit economics > CAC), high satisfaction (NPS, reviews), Retention, LTV.
- **C/D**: Margin < CAC, low satisfaction, high Churn, require lots of support resources.

## Real Criteria vs Consequences

- **Key question**: "What is it about the person that makes them satisfied and profitable?"
- **Consequences are not criteria**: "Bought for 10,000 rubles," "made 2+ orders," "0 complaints" --- these describe consequences, not the person. You need the cause.
- **Real criteria examples**: "willing to delegate everything," "large budget," "not first experience," "values time more than money."
- **Example (design studio)**: A --- investors from other cities with 100+ thousand/m2 budgets, willing to delegate. B --- local buyers with experience, lower budget. C --- want to do everything themselves. D --- first experience, unrealistic budget expectations.
- **Hidden factors**: Anxiety level, confidence in ability to do it independently, deadline presence, expected revenue from the project.
- **Budget thresholds**: Real criteria often include a specific budget threshold (e.g., "expected revenue of 1M rubles" --- willing to carve out a budget from that).

## Qualifying Questions from ABCD Criteria

- Knowing A/B/C/D characteristics, ask 4-5 questions to incoming leads to immediately classify them.
- **Examples** (design studio): First home? Experience with design/renovation? Budget per m2? What's more important --- time or money?
- **Example** (marketplace analytics): How many SKUs? Less than 5,000 --- not a fit. More than 5,000 --- transfer to sales.

## ABCD Is Unique for Each Product

- No two ABCD segmentations are the same --- it's segmentation through the lens of satisfaction with a specific product at a specific time.
- **Example**: Marketplace analytics that handles large SKU numbers --- A segment is sellers with huge SKU counts. For services that can't handle many SKUs, this same segment would be C.

## Two ABCD Segmentations for Marketplaces

- Marketplaces and intermediaries need TWO ABCD segmentations --- for buyers and for sellers.
- Select segments where the most efficient supply and demand serve each other.
- **Where to start**: In the Critical Chain, start with the scarcer side.

## How to Conduct ABCD Segmentation

1. **Collect data**: LTV, CAC, Retention, NPS, support requests, usage frequency.
2. **Calculate margin**: Margin per customer = LTV - CAC - Cost to serve.
3. **Assess satisfaction**: NPS, CSAT, reviews, Retention rate.
4. **Divide into groups**: High margin + high satisfaction = A. Low margin or low satisfaction = C/D.
5. **Analyze A/B by jobs**: наш подход interviews with A/B customers, identify segments by jobs within A/B.
6. **Focus on A/B, drop C/D**: Set up lead qualification to not attract C/D. Rebuild product/funnel for A/B.

## Strategy for Working with ABCD

1. Identify real criteria of A and B segments (causes, not consequences).
2. Focus marketing and sales only on A and B.
3. Stop listening to C/D feedback for product development.
4. Automate or raise prices for C/D to make them profitable or cut them off.

## Finding Segments Through Product Sales

- Segments can be found by selling the product --- profitable satisfied customers are an act of finding segments.
- **But the market isn't exhausted**: With 1% market share, the probability that you don't see all attractive segments is very high.
- **Need to acknowledge ignorance**: Go look both among paying customers and among competitor's customers.

## Research for Finding Segments Among Paying Customers

- **Who**: Companies with 100+ paying clients (B2B) or 1,000+ (B2C). High-ticket B2C: 100-200+.
- **Purpose**: Identify segments by jobs within A/B, rebuild communication, funnels, ads, sales scripts, onboardings.
- **Design**: ABCD segmentation -> наш подход interviews with A/B -> segment identification by jobs -> focus segment selection.
- **Output**: Segments/sub-segments by jobs among A/B, understanding of value, Aha-Moment, barriers, communication, product/acquisition optimization.

## Research for Finding Segments from Scratch

- **Who**: Companies without sales, with few sales, or looking for new segments outside current customers.
- **Design**: Take Big Job -> remove narrowing factors -> generate all solutions -> find people who paid competitor >X rubles, >Y times -> from different channels -> 60+ interviews -> "segment against segment" -> stop when 10 consecutive interviews yield no new jobs.
- **Selection criteria**: Pay >X rubles, do it Y times, match by company size/income. The fewer narrowing factors, the better.
- **Output**: Map of all segments, economic assessment, focus segment selection, reaction to your offer.

## Why Starting with A/B Is Beneficial

- Less risk (risky assumptions already verified).
- See returns faster (loyal customers, easier to sell).
- Easier to scale acquisition (known channels and creatives).
- Cheaper CAC (no need to explain from scratch).
- Local optimum, but fast and predictable growth.
