# Product Strategy

## Fundamental Thesis About Strategy

- **All strategic decisions = choosing jobs and segments**: "Which jobs of which people will we compete for, why these and not others, and why will we win the competition?"
- **Wrong jobs and segments = pain**: Incorrectly chosen jobs and segment cause a lot of pain. Correctly chosen ones are the key to differentiation, growth, and profit.
- **Segment is embedded in all decisions**: In every product decision (feature, launch, strategy), jobs and a specific segment are embedded --- always, without exception. Even if you don't realize it.

## The Problem of Function Synchronization

- **Research isn't integrated**: Researchers do research, product managers build features from stakeholder's head.
- **Product-marketing gap**: Product and researchers find segments, marketers don't know how to attract these people.
- **Feature-sales gap**: Implemented a feature, nobody understands how and to whom to sell it.
- **Focus loss**: Company does everything for everyone, no unified direction.
- **Function conflict**: Sales, marketing, product, and operations have contradicting goals.
- **Strategy disconnect**: Brand and marketing strategies created in isolation from product strategy.

### The Owner and Strategy Problem

- **Owner's risk**: The owner may make decisions outside the strategy or serve clients who don't fit.
- **Aura's protective factor**: When the whole company speaks one language and believes in a unified strategy, the owner argues not with one person, but with the entire company. Mistakes get noticed faster.

### наш подход as the Foundation of Synchronization

- **Everything stems from jobs of focus segments**: Product, value hypotheses, business model, acquisition channels --- everything is a derivative of the job.

## Definition of Strategy

- **Strategy = how to reach goals**: Not "become #1." Strategy is HOW to reach goals under conditions of limited resources and competition.
- **Strategy = sum of decisions**: Not just what executives decide at strategy sessions, but the sum of all daily decisions.
- **Strategy =/= goals**.
- **When strategy is needed**: Always (if you're in market conditions). Usually people look when growth stops, but you should search constantly.

## Diagnosis Before Strategy

- **The main mistake**: Lack of diagnosis and research at the start. CEOs skip analysis and go straight to solutions (Richard Rumelt, Good Strategy, Bad Strategy).
- **OODA cycle**: Observe (collect data) --- Orient (apply frameworks) --- Decide --- Act. Used by US Air Force.
- **Research for strategy**: Qualitative наш подход research + quantitative validation --- sufficient for 80-90% of strategic decisions.
- **Strategy is not secret knowledge, but an algorithm**: Critical chain, graph of jobs, graph movement, segment selection by 10 factors --- that IS the strategy.

## Data for Strategic Decisions

1. **Qualitative наш подход research**: Segments, jobs, graph of jobs (Core, Big, Small Jobs), competitors, Consideration Set, activating knowledge.
2. **Quantitative validation**: Survey of 300-500 people to assess segment sizes.
3. **Expert interviews**: With employees and competitors, competency analysis, strengths/weaknesses of players.
4. **Reports and market analytics**: Trends, regulation, forecasts.
5. **Competency analysis**: What resources, technologies, competencies we and competitors have.

## Algorithm for Product Decision-Making

1. **Diagnostics**: Analyze current state across all components (segments, jobs, value, unit economics, channels, funnel).
2. **Define the business task**: Launch, scale, grow conversion, exit competition, retain customers.
3. **Hypothesize strategies**: Which mechanics could solve this task (from 72+ strategies).
4. **Do research**: Which theoretical strategies are actually applicable in the market.
5. **Apply strategies to graphs**: Over actual graphs and segments, get a list of hypotheses.
6. **Rank by RICE**: Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort.
7. **Check risks (RAT)**: Identify riskiest assumptions, verify cheaply and quickly.
8. **Implement**: If risks pass --- build and launch.

## Fractality of Strategies

- The same strategy works at different levels --- from micro-feature to company strategy. Example: "Enter the next job" can be a button in the UI or a corporate strategy.
- Once you understand the mechanics (kill the job, simplify, combine), you apply them everywhere --- from copywriting to market selection.

## Mechanics vs Strategies

- **Mechanics**: Mandatory actions (what we must do).
- **Strategies**: Alternatives and choices (what we can choose to do or not).
- Example: "direct attention" is a mechanic; "came for one thing --- offer another" (Google, game launchers) is a strategy built on it.

## Building Features vs Knowing Strategies

- **Building features IS a strategy**: If you've spent a year building features and fixing pains --- that's your (default) strategy.
- **Knowing the market changes the game**: When you know segments, graph of jobs, all possible mechanics and strategies --- you outpace those who only build features.

## Strategies as the Course Foundation

- **~80 strategies** in the full course, ~30 in the basic module.
- **Different strategies for different tasks**: Launch, scaling, conversion growth, competition exit.
- **Example scaling strategies**: Add adjacent segment, shift segment, add B2B to B2C, scale within segment, enter different job, shift to segment by budget.

## Strategies Tell You What to Look For

- Strategies show where in the market there's potentially a solution to your business task.
- Each strategy indicates which people to find, which questions to ask, which experts to talk to.

## Developing an Existing Product vs Launching a New One

### Fundamental Difference

- **You already have something working**: Product, value, channels, some business model.
- **Main risks already reduced**: The fundamental risks (wrong segment, wrong value) are significantly reduced.
- **Remaining problems**: Maybe not the most attractive segment, not enough value, poor communication. But it somehow works.

### Focus Shift: From Search to Scaling

- **New product stage**: Find segment, find value, achieve first sales.
- **Existing product stage**: Scaling, acquisition efficiency, service efficiency, strategic development.

### Constraints When Developing an Existing Product

- **Organizational**: Hard to change business model, segment, value because "it somehow works."
- **Customer**: Paying customers have expectations. Which jobs they hire you for strongly influences your strategy.
- **PM scope**: Limited scope of responsibility can make it hard to address suboptimalities outside your area.

### The "If It Works --- Don't Touch It" Trap

- **The Innovator's Dilemma** (Clayton Christensen): Fear of losing current revenue prevents investing in the global optimum.
- **сегментация is the answer**: Christensen laid the foundations of сегментация in part to escape the innovator's dilemma.

### Christensen's Discovery

- Large companies that get disrupted **don't work badly** --- they work well. What prevents them from adapting is that they're too focused on current customers.
- New products look like toys. They say "this will never work" because current customers aren't interested.

### сегментация as a Way Out

- **Graph of jobs = exit tool**: Entering previous/next jobs, shifting to new segments, entering jobs at a higher level.

## Scaling and Competition Exit Strategies

- **Lock in unique value**: Exclusive content (Spotify + Joe Rogan, Disney+ + Marvel).
- **Direct customer attention**: Insert products/ads into the customer's critical chain (news sites, game launchers, Google).
- **Enter the previous job**: Product/content for the job BEFORE Core Job (aggregators, guides, calculators).
- **Enter the job above**: Kill jobs at a lower level, rise to the Big Job.
- **Shift/add segments**: Horizontal expansion to adjacent segments.
- **Up-market / Down-market**: Price shift to premium or mass market.
- **B2B <-> B2C**: Transition on the same technologies.
- **Super App**: Perform other customer jobs unrelated to the original Big Job (Yandex.Go, WeChat).
- **Ecosystem**: Link multiple products for unique combined value.

## Timelines for Strategic Research

- **Longest**: ~1 year (many markets + long internal approval).
- **Shortest**: 1.5-2 months.
- **Average B2C**: 3-5 months.
- **B2B**: Depends on recruiting complexity.

## Causal Chain to Profit

1. Enough money in the market for our ambitions
2. Sufficiently large customer segments with jobs for which we can create added value
3. Target margin per unit is theoretically achievable + acquisition channels exist
4. Customers buy our product and we know the value
5. On a flow of customers, we achieve target margin per unit
6. We attract the needed customers at target cost and quality
7. We convert them into payment / repeat payments
8. We achieve target profit

## Value Communication

- Value communication MUST start with knowing the jobs of the target segment.
- If you've done наш подход research and know Big Jobs, Core Jobs, value and barriers --- you already have everything needed for landing page texts, sales scripts, creatives, and lead magnets.

## Final Algorithm "How to Build a Product" (Aura)

1. **See all product components** (Aura's causal chain).
2. **Identify all assumptions** (risky assumptions).
3. **Verify from the highest risk**: Biggest risk, highest error cost, cheapest verification.
4. **Strategy algorithm**: Define business task -> hypothesize mechanics/strategies -> research -> apply to graphs -> rank by RICE -> check risks (RAT) -> implement.
5. **Business tasks**: Grow value, launch, scale, exit competition, acquire customers, grow conversion, create channels, grow average check, retain customers.

## Existential Answer: How to Build a Product

- **On a years horizon**: Products constantly kill jobs and rise to ever higher job levels.
- **Constant graph simplification**: Each new generation makes the graph simpler --- fewer jobs to achieve the same Big Job.
- **Example**: Point A to B. Before: buy car, fill gas, find route, park. Now: open app, press button.
- **Bundling and unbundling**: Products alternately split into narrow solutions and reassemble into Super Apps.
