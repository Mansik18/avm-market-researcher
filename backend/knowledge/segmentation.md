# Customer Segmentation by Jobs

## Segmentation Fundamentals

### Definition and Multiplicity

- **Definition**: Customer segmentation is grouping customers by any attribute.
- **Many types**: There are infinite types of segmentation. All segmentations serve different purposes.

### Key Principle: Jobs Come First

- **First criterion --- job graph similarity**: Not demographics, not age/gender/income, but similarity of people's job graphs. If people perform similar jobs --- they are one segment.
- **Demographics are secondary**: Used only as a correlate (e.g., "wealthy" is a consequence, the cause is "willingness to pay for time savings").
- **No "average customer"**: Creating a product for the "average" user results in a product for nobody. One customer in one context belongs to only one segment.

### Causal Chain to Profit

**In segmentation context:** The chain works from money in the market → narrows to segments where we can create added value → value = performing their jobs more efficiently. If people with these jobs don't exist --- there won't be a product.

### Choosing a Job Determines Fate

- **Key strategic decision**: "Which jobs of which people we compete for" determines everything: strategy, positioning, differentiation, product development.

## Real vs False Segmentation

- **Real segmentation**: Segmentation that enables decision-making and leads to business value.
- **False segmentation**: Creates a supposed sense of understanding ("here are our segments"), but doesn't participate in decisions and doesn't lead to business value.
- **Scary truth**: In every decision we embed segments and jobs, often without realizing it.

## Basic Scaling Strategy Through Segments

- **Basic strategy**: Niche into one segment, grow within it, then add adjacent segments. Consciously reject the attempt to serve everyone.
- **Process**: Don't know segments -> research (different people with different jobs) -> evaluate budgets and satisfaction -> choose by factors -> create value -> create funnel -> optimize.
- **For existing products**: Same process, but add adjacent segments to existing ones.
- **Yandex.Taxi example**: Launched with Economy, then added Comfort, Comfort+, Business, Ultima --- each tier entering an adjacent segment with different jobs.
- **Yura Vesnevsky case (Sky)**: Forgot his segment ideas, conducted 20-30 interviews, found an underserved segment --- product drove 75% revenue growth across all company products.
- **ARTDENT case**: Focused on a specific segment by jobs --- got +37% revenue.
- **Best approach**: Forget your segment hypotheses. You may be unconsciously confirming a distorted worldview.

## Growth Algorithm Within a Segment by Core Jobs

- **Step 1: Create value for the segment**: First, create value --- more efficiently perform Core Jobs of the focus segment.
- **Step 2: Create an acquisition funnel**: Set up acquisition that hits the jobs and success criteria of the focus segment.
- **Step 3: Pump money into the funnel**: Invest budget in acquisition through the configured funnel.
- **Step 4: Grow the funnel's ROMI**: Optimize the funnel so return on marketing investment grows (example: +40% ROMI).

## Sub-segments and Growth Through Sub-segments

- **Within a segment there are many sub-segments**: Different contexts and additional specifics. Initially, everything is tuned for one sub-segment.
- **Growth**: Learn sub-segments, add second and third --- adapting acquisition, funnels, and product for their specifics.
- **Example (real estate)**: "Buy an apartment for rental income" contains sub-segments: "anxious about developers going bankrupt," "rent is hard to pay," "wants a specific district," "has maternity capital," "works in IT."

## Scaling a Product to +1 Segment

- **Principle**: Scale the product to +1 segment for fundamentally the same Core Jobs.
- **Yandex.Taxi example**: Started with Economy ("Get from A to B cheaply"), added Comfort ("Get from A to B comfortably"), then Ultima ("Get from A to B with prestige and comfort"). The Core Job is the same (get from A to B), but success criteria are different.

## Signs of Not Knowing Segments by Jobs

- Building and selling features instead of segment jobs.
- Can't articulate what value we create for which segment.
- Salespeople can't hit the customer's jobs, communication doesn't resonate.
- Everything for everyone = nothing for nobody.

## Three Key Segmentation Mistakes

- **Mistake 1: Product for non-existent jobs**: Embedded non-existent jobs -> sales absent or very weak. Solved by studying segments.
- **Mistake 2: Small, unprofitable segments**: Segment is unprofitable (small budget, rare jobs). A bigger segment may be nearby.
- **Mistake 3: Resource dilution**: Features built for other people's jobs. The team does "everything for everyone."
- **Consequences**: Lost money, lost time (more painful), lost years, no go-to-market.

## Habit, Barriers, and Four Forces of Progress in Segmentation Context

### Applying the 4 Forces for Segment Selection

- **Choose the segment that hurts most**: Where problems with the current solution are greatest.
- **Account for barriers and habit**: All else being equal, choose a segment with lower barriers and weaker habit toward the current solution.

## Typical Mistakes When Finding and Choosing a Segment

### Mistake 1: Skip Market Assessment

- **The mistake**: Starting research without assessing the market. Two years later you discover revenue could be 10x greater in a different market.
- **Vova Lazarev case**: Tool for researchers. The most important job turned out to be finding clients, not how the researcher works.
- **Kolya Bobrov case**: Industrial 3D printers in Russia --- market is hundreds of millions of rubles, not billions.
- **Rule**: Assess the market (how much money is there) before research.

### Mistake 2: Interviewing People Who Don't Pay

- **The mistake**: Choosing respondents who fundamentally cannot be profitable.
- **Examples**: Premium pet food -> interviewing cheap pet food buyers. B2B software -> interviewing users of free competitors.
- **Rule**: Don't look for segments among people who currently don't pay. They likely won't start paying.

### Mistake 3: Thinking We Know Everything

- **The mistake**: "It's all clear what needs to be done." The hallucination that we know what jobs exist is the #1 cause of product failure. Decisions based on invented segments carry the risk of being completely wrong.

## Traps of Typical Decisions

- **Churned customers**: You implicitly segment into never paid / paid and stayed / paid and churned --- but not necessarily correctly.
- **Non-form-completers**: May not contain a single representative of the profitable segment.
- **Complainers**: May be a small, unprofitable C/D segment while a large A/B segment is nearby.
- **Rule**: Every decision must include: (1) Jobs, (2) Unit margin, (3) Segment focus.

## Real vs False Segmentation Criteria

- **False segmentation**: Creates an illusion of understanding but doesn't help decisions. Example (real estate): Economy/comfort/business/premium --- a student buys luxury, an entrepreneur buys 20 economy apartments. Where's the job?
- **Criteria sequence**: First segment by jobs, then add criteria. ABCD exception: margin + satisfaction comes first, then jobs.
- **Jobs should never be removed or placed second** (except in ABCD).

## Three Factors for Segment Selection

1. **Ability to create added value**:
   - Can we perform this segment's jobs more efficiently than competitors?
   - Do we have the resources for this?
2. **Ability to earn target unit profit (margin)**:
   - Does the segment have budget, job frequency, transaction volume, employee costs?
3. **Ability to scale in the segment**:
   - Segment size (enough people).
   - Ability to create demand.
   - Accessibility in channels (Meta, Yandex, conferences, partner channels).

## "Segment / Not Segment" Exercise

### Exercise Goal

- **For each respondent**: Evaluate each job of each person for the ability to create added value and extract real segmentation criteria.
- **Classification**: Place the person in one of two buckets: "segment" (fits) and "not segment" (can't serve).

### When to Apply

- **During research**: After each interview. In each iteration after conducting 6-8 interviews.

### Exercise Process

- **Step 1: Segment / Not Segment**: Sort customer jobs into folders.
- **Step 2: Within segments --- by job similarity**: Within the "segment" folder, identify segments by job similarity.
- **Step 3: Supplement in iterations**: In each subsequent iteration, also do segment/not segment and supplement the folders.

### Four Exercise Questions

1. **Can we deliver added value?** Yes/No. If "Yes" -> What? What about this person/company makes this value valuable for them?
2. **Can we earn target unit profit?** Yes/No. What about this person/company?
3. **Is the segment large enough?** Yes/No.
4. **Can we create demand?** Yes/No. Are there acquisition channels?

If all answers are "Yes" -> goes into the segment bucket.

### Critical: Don't Stay at the Consequence Level

- **Problem**: "They'll earn an extra $2,000 in a year" is rephrased value, not a person's characteristic.
- **Correct**: "What about this person makes $2,000 valuable?" -> "They understand inflation erodes capital, 10-50x is unrealistic, this is better than a deposit, stable and risk-free."
- **Rule**: Dig from consequences to causes.

### Application Examples

- **Food delivery**: Value --- fresh vegetables. Real criterion: vegetarians --- this is the foundation of their diet.
- **Task tracker**: Value --- detailed productivity reports. Real criterion: outsourcer, profit directly depends on productivity.
- **Crypto wallet**: Value --- portfolio control. Real criterion: already invests in stocks, understands that risky assets require control.

### Exercise Result

- **A set of jobs with identical success criteria**
- **Real segmentation criteria**: Explain why the person has these jobs and clarify which jobs of which segments are more efficient to compete for.

## Examples of Real Segmentation Criteria

- **For advertising automation**: Employee cost >2500 EUR, many orders and clients, employees burn out.
- **For AI training**: Small/medium businesses, FOMO about AI, cost of hiring an expert too high, specific need to grow X% through AI.
- **For press office (PR)**: Large companies with multiple verticals, more than X articles per year, more than Y crises, high reputational risks.
- **For trading analytics**: Trading volumes >X billion rubles, number of operations >Y, penalty damage significantly exceeds product cost.
- **For hotels**: Revenue/occupancy above threshold, room price above X, hotel margin allows budget allocation.
- **For in-app communications**: Complex applications (not catalogs), high communication needs, good client unit economics.

## From Value to Segmentation Criteria

- **Value vs criterion**: "Faster creative selection" is value. "Many orders and clients" is a criterion (why the value is valuable).
- **Consequence vs cause**: "Generate more leads" is a consequence. "High employee cost" is the cause why faster = valuable.
- **Why know jobs?**: To understand for which jobs of which people we can create greater value.

## Segment Naming

- **"Reverse order" trap**: Saw a trait ("entrepreneurs") -> named the segment -> spend 5 years building a product, forgetting about jobs.
- **Correct order**: (1) Jobs, (2) Real segmentation criteria, (3) A short and memorable name derived from jobs and criteria.
- **Name is a label for a set of jobs + criteria**: Not just "Sellers," but "Pet product sellers with >10M/month turnover, operating in 5+ regions."

## Anti-segments (Mirror Description)

- **Mirror evil twin**: For each segment criterion, you can create an anti-segment --- people with opposite characteristics.
- **Example**: Many orders and clients -> Few orders and few clients. Will they buy? No.
- **Criteria verification**: Anti-segments help verify whether criteria are correctly identified.

## False Attributes

- "People don't want to deal with technical routine" --- too general. Dig deeper: because employee cost >2500 EUR.
- Country/demographics as first criterion often turn out to be false if not connected to jobs and value.

## Focus Segment

- **Definition**: The segment whose jobs you've chosen to compete for. Chosen by: learn segments -> discard unprofitable -> choose one focus.
- **Why focus**: Resources are finite. Thin layer for everyone = a drop of value. Focus = "wow" for one segment --- performing jobs dramatically faster, simpler, cheaper.
- **Profit -> expansion**: Use profit from focus segment to build for the next segment.

## Segment Finding Process (Methodology)

### First Rule: Don't Validate a Narrow Job Hypothesis

- **Example of narrow hypothesis**: "An assistant for product requirement documents in US tech startups" --- too narrow.
- **How to expand**: Remove geo, remove PRD, remove "tech giants." Leave "some employee writes some complex document."
- **Why**: A criterion broad enough to capture different segments, among which there will most likely be those for whom you can create unique value.

### Research Principles

- **Goal**: Find what segments with what jobs exist and where we can create unique value.
- **Recruit by the broadest, highest-level job criterion**, from different channels to avoid biased samples.
- **Iterate in batches of 6-8 interviews**: Not 20-30 at once. After each batch: discuss -> adjust guide -> adjust recruitment -> continue. Information after 30 interviews may significantly change direction.

## Research for Finding Segments Among Paying Customers

- **Who it suits**: Companies with 100+ paying customers (B2B) or 1000+ (B2C).
- **Goal**: Identify segments by jobs within A/B customers.
- **Design**: ABCD segmentation -> наш подход interviews with A/B -> identify segments by jobs -> choose focus ones.
- **What you learn**: Core Jobs with success criteria, Big Jobs, barriers, product value, Aha-moment.

## Research for Finding Segments from Scratch

- **Who it suits**: Companies without sales or seeking new segments outside current customers.
- **Goal**: Find segments in the market that pay for the Big Job.
- **Design**: Take a Big Job -> remove narrowing factors -> find everyone who pays for the Big Job -> 60+ interviews -> stop when 10 consecutive interviews yield no new jobs.
- **Sample criteria**: Pay more than X rubles, do Y times, fit by size.

## Why Starting with A/B Is Beneficial

- Less risk (assumptions already validated), faster returns (product known), easier acquisition scaling, cheaper CAC.

## Why Finding New Segments Is Important

- Missing attractive segments (1% market share), competitors may find them first, new segment can deliver x2-x10 growth, exit from direct competition, reduced dependency risk.

## Additional Segment Selection Mechanics

### Mechanic: Choose an Opinion Leader Segment

- **Essence**: Choose a segment that is an opinion leader for other segments --- can reduce acquisition costs through recommendations and authority.

### Mechanic: Viral Jobs

- **Definition**: Find a customer segment with viral jobs --- jobs performed for other people or together with other people. This provides cheap or free acquisition.

### Mechanic: Segment That May Grow by Trends

- **Essence**: Look at which segments may grow by trends. A small segment now may significantly grow in the future.

### Mechanic: Fast Feedback Cycles

- **Essence**: All else being equal, choose segments whose feedback cycles come significantly faster --- per unit of time you can test significantly more hypotheses.

### Anti-mechanic: DON'T Choose an Easily Replicable Job

- **Essence**: Don't choose a job that a major player can easily replicate and activate their customers for.

### Mechanic: Growth Points Outside Current Jobs

- **Growth points lie outside current jobs**: Need to not only better perform jobs that the current solution is hired for, but also study the job graph around current solutions' Core Jobs.
- **How to escape the innovator's dilemma?**: Study the job graph and apply strategic mechanics.

## How to Choose a Focus Segment

- **10 selection factors**: Size, Job budget, Job frequency, Accessibility, Pain, Competition, Ability to create value, Sustainable competitive advantage, Strategic value, Team's personal motivation.
- **Score by points**: Each factor on a 10-point scale, compare segments.
- **Qualitative + quantitative**: Qualitative (interviews) gives understanding of jobs, quantitative (survey of 300-500 people) gives size estimates.
- **One focus segment**: At launch, focus on one.

## наш подход for Internal Products

### Methodology Applicability

- **наш подход works great for internal tools**: Task Tracker, CRM, A/B testing tool, order processing tool.
- **There are nuances**: (1) Which segments we serve, (2) What value means for internal products, (3) How to conduct interviews.

### Segmentation: Budget Allocation Motivation

- **Understand the business goal**: What motivates stakeholders to allocate money for internal products?
- **Three types of optimization**:
  1. **Expensive employee optimization**: Developers, analysts. Greater focus on value creation.
  2. **Mass employee optimization**: Line salespeople, call centers. Optimization at scale.
  3. **Key service optimization**: Services generating primary profit.

### ABCD Segmentation Applies to Employees

- **Kidsway case**: A very small percentage of employees with cars make 80% of all trips. Identify employees for whom investment is more efficient.

### Value and Currency of Internal Products

- **Situation 1: Not administratively forced**: Identical to market conditions. Value criterion: the end employee switched from the current solution to yours.
- **Situation 2: Administratively imposed**: Imposed monopoly corrupts. Basic criterion: customers using the product don't suffer.

### What Interviews to Conduct

- **Decision-makers and expensive employees**: B2B-style interview (business + personal jobs).
- **Line employees**: B2C-style interview. Main motivation: making life easier.
- **Approach**: Find valuable employees -> interviews -> job graph -> value hypothesis -> create -> test.
